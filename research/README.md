## Research ethics

**Design.** The experiment observes a public, permissionless system. The free test credits have no
monetary value and no price is promised, so participants cannot lose money by taking part; they pay
only their own network fees. No one is paid, recruited under false pretences or asked to act against
their interest.

**On-chain data.** Transactions on Arbitrum One are public by design. Analysis uses them in aggregate
(counts, timing, outcomes). Wallets other than the founder's are not named in publications, and
transaction hashes that would identify them are removed from published screenshots.

**Site analytics.** Google Analytics loads only after a visitor accepts analytics cookies. It
receives step names (connect, verify, claim, bid, settle, vote) and public listing numbers, never
wallet addresses, amounts or other personal data.

**Social media and messages.** Public posts are studied anonymised. Private messages sent to the
project are described only in aggregate or anonymised, with accounts labelled A, B, C…; names,
handles, avatars and follower counts are removed. The founder replied to one message to let a sales
pitch run to its end, without misrepresenting himself, promising payment or accepting any offer.

**Invited testers.** Testers invited in person are described anonymously, and only with their
written consent. Consent from the first tester (25 October 2026) has been requested; the second
(10 November 2026) gave consent on 13 November 2026.

**Researcher's role.** The founder designed the system, operates it and analyses it. This conflict
of interest is disclosed: the hypotheses were pre-registered before any data were collected, every
observation is logged with its date and limits in [OBSERVATIONS.md](OBSERVATIONS.md), and results
that do not support the hypotheses will be published as well.

**Review.** If the findings are submitted through a university or a journal, approval or an exemption
from the relevant ethics committee will be sought before submission, and this section updated.
